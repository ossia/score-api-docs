◆ begin_tick()

void mtk::MIDISyncOut::begin_tick ( int  frames)
inlinenoexcept

Dates the frames of the tick for the device: the start of the tick follows the previous one by its length, corrected slowly towards the time operator() is called, so that the jitter of the audio callback does not reach the clock. One buffer of latency puts every message of the tick in the future of the clock thread.

◆ clock_thread_main()

void mtk::MIDISyncOut::clock_thread_main ( )
inline

The clock thread: runs the free clock, and writes to the MIDI device what operator() dated for it. Every message is sent at its own date, from the steady clock: waiting never adds up from one message to the next.

◆ midi_out

ossia::net::midi::midi_stream* mtk::MIDISyncOut::midi_out {}

The MIDI device the output is bound to, as of the last tick: audio thread only. The clock thread gets its own copy through to_device, in order with the messages, and lets go of it when the process stops or pauses: a process that is not ticked cannot tell when its device is removed. In Free mode the clock then sends to the output port until the next tick.

◆ to_device

ossia::spsc_queue<device_message> mtk::MIDISyncOut::to_device {4096}

What operator() makes when the output is a MIDI device: score clock, transport and timecode, dated in steady_clock nanoseconds. The clock thread writes each to the device at its date; the output port stays empty, else the graph would write everything a second time, in a burst at the end of the tick. A full queue drops messages.

◆ to_output_port

ossia::spsc_queue<midi_clock::message> mtk::MIDISyncOut::to_output_port {1024}

What the free clock sends when the output has no MIDI device to write to: operator() forwards it through the output port, and drops what waited longer than a tick or two: nothing drains it while the process is not ticked.
